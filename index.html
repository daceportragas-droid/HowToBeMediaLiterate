01 — Concept

What counts as cyberbullying

Cyberbullying is the use of digital platforms — social media, messaging apps, online games — to harass, threaten, embarrass, or spread harmful information about another person.

In Media and Information Literacy, it's treated as a misuse of digital media: a reminder that responsible, ethical communication online is a skill, not an afterthought.

02 — Ground rules

Netiquette: the etiquette that keeps disagreement from escalating

Network etiquette — the social and ethical rules of digital spaces. Screens lower empathy by default, so these rules exist to put it back.

Remember the human

There's always a person on the receiving end of a message — communicating from behind a screen makes that easy to forget.

Don't feed the escalation

Avoid joining or fueling online shouting matches, arguments, and personal attacks.

Watch your tone

Skip ALL CAPS and aggressive language — tone is easy to misread in text, and shouting reads exactly as it sounds.

Respect privacy

Never share private photos, personal details, or private conversations without consent.

Be an upstander, not a bystander

Report abuse, block harassers, and support the people targeted — passivity lets harm continue.

Model the norm

Respectful communication is contagious — practicing it directly lowers how often cyberbullying happens in a group.

03 — Consequences

What cyberbullying does to the person on the other end

Depression Anxiety Chronic stress Low self-esteem Self-harm Suicidality

Depression and anxiety often follow when victims struggle to regulate intense emotions, spiraling into self-blame and excessive worry.

Stress and low self-esteem build when repeated harassment leaves someone feeling isolated and overwhelmed, reshaping how they see themselves.

At the most serious end, sustained harassment can lead to self-injurious behavior and suicidal thoughts — which is why early intervention matters.

04 — Intervention

From bystander to upstander

Recognizing digital abuse is only step one — the second is choosing to act instead of scroll past.

Direct

Call out the abusive behavior or defend the target, if it's safe to do so.

Indirect

Report the post, block the account, or flag the behavior to platform moderators.

Document

Screenshot abusive messages or posts so proof exists for authorities or school administrators.

Support

Offer the person being targeted private empathy and resources — it softens the psychological toll.

06 — Empowerment

Five steps, in order, if it happens to you

Don't walk into it

Keep exchanges professional and respectful. If a game chat or a comment thread is getting heated, step back before it escalates.

Try to de-escalate

If harassment is starting, talk it out where possible — disagreements can spiral fast, so address it early rather than letting it build.

Cut off contact and get evidence

Once it's no longer discourse but active harassment: block, report, and save clear evidence. Tell someone you trust.

Use the platform's tools

Most platforms have reporting protocols built for exactly this — reporting directly from the platform often stops the harassment outright.

Escalate to the authorities

For defamation, threats, or leaked personal information, report to the police or a local anti-cybercrime unit — in the Philippines, the PNP's Anti-Cybercrime Group.
